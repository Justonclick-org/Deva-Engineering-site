Sustainability

Energy that starts in the field and returns to it

Biogas connects agriculture, waste management and energy. Done well, it turns organic material into renewable fuel and gives nutrients back to the soil.

Circular economy

A closed loop from soil to fuel to soil

In a Napier-grass-based CBG plant, the same land that grows the feedstock can receive the organic manure recovered from digestate. Energy leaves the loop as CBG; organic matter and nutrients return.

Circular biogas value chainNapier grass cultivationHarvest & feedstock supplyBiogas → CBGFOM / LFOM to farmland
Our contribution

Where biogas creates sustainable value

Renewable energy generation

Biogas is produced from recently grown biomass and organic residues. Upgraded to CBG, it provides a renewable gaseous fuel.

Agricultural biomass utilisation

Energy crops such as Napier grass and crop residues become a productive feedstock — and a potential additional income stream for farmers.

Organic waste valorisation

Organic material that would otherwise be burned, landfilled or left to decompose is converted into energy and manure.

Digestate recovery

Digestate is separated into fermented organic manure (FOM) and liquid fermented organic manure (LFOM), returning organic matter and nutrients to the soil.

Reduced fossil fuel dependence

Compressed biogas can substitute fossil-derived gas in suitable applications, contributing to lower dependence on imported fossil fuels.

Local value creation

Plants located near feedstock create rural employment in cultivation, logistics, operations and manure distribution.

We do not publish carbon-saving or environmental-impact figures until they can be measured or verified for an operating plant. Benefits described here are qualitative.
