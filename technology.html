Technology & process

From Napier grass to compressed biogas

Feedstock → pre-treatment → slurry → anaerobic digestion → raw biogas → H₂S removal → CO₂ upgrading → drying → compression → CBG filling. With digestate recovered as organic manure.

Stage by stage

What happens at each stage

Concise technical notes for each step of the gas pathway.

01

Feedstock harvesting & receipt

Napier grass is harvested and brought to the plant, supplemented by other organic feedstock under the multi-feedstock design. The design basis is 115 TPD at 25% total solids.

  • Napier grass harvesting
  • Multi-feedstock flexibility
  • Weighbridge & storage
02

Chopping, grinding & conveying

Grass is chopped and ground to reduce particle size, increasing the surface area available to microorganisms, then conveyed to slurry preparation.

  • Grass chopping
  • Grinding / size reduction
  • Feedstock conveying
03

Slurry preparation

Prepared biomass is mixed with process liquid — including recirculated liquid from digestate separation — to form a pumpable slurry fed to the digester.

  • Mixing with process water
  • LFOM recirculation
  • Controlled feeding
04

Mesophilic anaerobic digestion

A single 10,000 m³ continuously stirred tank reactor (CSTR) digests the slurry under mesophilic conditions with a hydraulic retention time of approximately 36 days.

  • 1 × 10,000 m³ CSTR
  • Mesophilic regime
  • HRT ≈ 36 days
05

Raw biogas collection

Biogas is collected from the digester headspace. Design production is 11,900–13,200 Nm³/day at 55–60% methane, the balance being mainly CO₂ with traces of H₂S and moisture.

  • 11,900–13,200 Nm³/day
  • 55–60% CH₄
  • Gas collection & handling
06

Hydrogen sulphide removal

Water washing / scrubbing and iron oxyhydroxide media remove H₂S, protecting compressors and pipework from corrosion.

  • Water washing / scrubbing
  • Iron oxyhydroxide media
  • Corrosion protection
07

CO₂ removal & methane upgrading

Chilled-water scrubbing or two-stage membrane technology separates CO₂, upgrading the gas to at least 98% methane.

  • Chilled-water scrubbing
  • or two-stage membranes
  • ≥ 98% CH₄ product
08

Gas drying

A desiccant dryer with heatless regeneration removes moisture to the dew point required for high-pressure storage.

  • Desiccant dryer
  • Heatless regeneration
  • Dew-point control
09

High-pressure compression & odourisation

Reciprocating compressors raise the gas to a design pressure of up to 250 bar. The gas is odourised so that any leak can be detected by smell.

  • Reciprocating compressor
  • Up to 250 bar
  • Odourisation
10

CBG cascade filling & dispatch

Compressed biogas is stored and dispatched through cascade filling. PLC/SCADA and online gas analysers monitor quality throughout.

  • Cascade filling
  • Online gas quality monitoring
  • PLC / SCADA
Anaerobic digestion

Mesophilic CSTR digestion

A continuously stirred tank reactor keeps feedstock, microorganisms and temperature uniform throughout the tank. Our flagship design uses a single 10,000 m³ CSTR in the mesophilic range with a hydraulic retention time of approximately 36 days.

Gas purification & upgrading

Gas cleaning, upgrading and compression

The purification configuration specified in the DPR. These technologies are supplied by specialist technology partners and are not proprietary to Deva Bio Engineering.

H₂S removal

Water washing / scrubbing with iron oxyhydroxide media

Hydrogen sulphide is removed early in the gas train to protect downstream equipment from corrosion and to meet CBG quality limits.

CO₂ removal & methane upgrading

Chilled-water scrubbing or two-stage membrane separation

Carbon dioxide is separated from the gas stream to raise methane content from 55–60% in raw biogas to at least 98% in the product gas.

Gas drying

Desiccant dryer with heatless regeneration

Moisture is removed so the gas meets dew-point requirements before high-pressure compression and storage.

High-pressure compression

Reciprocating compressor, design up to 250 bar

Upgraded gas is compressed in stages for cascade storage and dispatch as compressed biogas.

Automation & gas quality

PLC / SCADA with online gas quality monitoring

Process parameters and product gas composition are monitored continuously for stable, safe and documented operation.

Product gas

≥ 98% CH₄ · up to 250 bar · odourised

The result is compressed biogas suitable for cascade storage and dispatch, monitored continuously by online gas analysers.

Compression & filling

High-pressure compression and cascade filling

Dried, upgraded gas is compressed by reciprocating compressors to a design pressure of up to 250 bar, odourised, and stored in cylinder cascades from which CBG is dispatched.

Digestate pathway

Digestion → separation → FOM / LFOM

Digestate leaving the CSTR is separated into solid and liquid fractions.

  • Digestate discharge — Digested material leaves the CSTR as digestate, containing undigested fibre, nutrients and process water.
  • Solid–liquid separation — Digestate is mechanically separated into a solid fraction and a liquid fraction.
  • Fermented Organic Manure — The solid fraction is recovered as Fermented Organic Manure (FOM) for soil application after conditioning.
  • Liquid Fermented Organic Manure — The liquid fraction (LFOM) is partly recirculated to slurry preparation and partly available as a liquid organic nutrient.

Actual FOM and LFOM quantities and their marketable quality depend on feedstock, plant operation and final product specifications, including applicable fertiliser regulations.
