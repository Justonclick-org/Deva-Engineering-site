The basics

How biogas works

Biogas is produced when microorganisms break down organic matter without oxygen. Upgraded and compressed, it becomes a renewable fuel.

Step 1

Organic matter

Energy crops, crop residues, manure or organic waste provide carbon-rich material.

Step 2

Anaerobic digestion

Microorganisms break it down in a sealed, oxygen-free digester at a controlled temperature.

Step 3

Raw biogas

The process releases biogas — mostly methane and carbon dioxide — plus digestate.

Step 4

Compressed biogas

Cleaned, upgraded and compressed, biogas becomes CBG: a renewable gaseous fuel.

Digestate value chain

Nothing wasted: organic manure from digestate

After digestion, the remaining material is separated into Fermented Organic Manure (FOM) and Liquid Fermented Organic Manure (LFOM).

  • FOM — the solid fraction, conditioned as an organic soil amendment.
  • LFOM — the liquid fraction, partly recirculated to slurry preparation.
  • Back to the farm — manure can return to the fields that grow the Napier grass.

Actual FOM and LFOM quantities and their marketable quality depend on feedstock, plant operation and final product specifications, including applicable fertiliser regulations.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Common questions about compressed biogas, Napier grass feedstock and our flagship project.

Ask us a question

CBG is biogas that has been cleaned and upgraded to a high methane content and then compressed for storage and transport. In our flagship design the product gas has at least 98% methane and is compressed to a design pressure of up to 250 bar, making it comparable in use to compressed natural gas.

Napier grass is chopped, ground and mixed into a slurry, which is fed to an anaerobic digester. Microorganisms break down the organic matter without oxygen and release biogas — mainly methane and carbon dioxide. The gas is then cleaned, upgraded, dried and compressed into CBG.

Napier grass is a high-yield perennial grass that can be harvested several times a year, giving a plant a plannable feedstock base. It can be grown by local farmers, creating an additional income source, and our plant design also allows other organic feedstock to be used alongside it.

A continuously stirred tank reactor (CSTR) is a digester in which the contents are mixed so that feedstock, microorganisms and temperature stay uniform. Our flagship project uses a single 10,000 m³ CSTR operating in the mesophilic temperature range with a hydraulic retention time of about 36 days.

Digestate is separated into a solid fraction, Fermented Organic Manure (FOM), and a liquid fraction, Liquid Fermented Organic Manure (LFOM). Part of the liquid is recirculated to slurry preparation. Actual quantities and quality depend on feedstock and plant operation.
